Minecraft Java settings for more FPS (what each one actually costs)

Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

Lower Render Distance first, then Simulation Distance, set Graphics to Fast, and make sure Max Framerate isn't below your monitor's refresh rate. Clouds, particles and biome blend are smaller wins. For a much bigger jump, a performance mod like Sodium beats any setting.

Minecraft is unusual: a lot of its cost is on the CPU, building chunks and ticking the world, not just drawing pixels. That's why some settings that look heavy barely matter, and some that look harmless cost a lot. Press F3 in game to see your FPS in the top-left corner while you try these, and test in the same spot each time.

1. Render Distance (biggest lever)

Every extra chunk of distance adds a ring of chunks to build and draw, so the cost grows fast. Going from 16 to 10 chunks is often the single largest FPS gain you can get. 8 to 12 is a sensible range on laptops; fog just starts closer.

2. Simulation Distance

How far from you the world keeps ticking: mobs, crops, redstone. This is pure CPU work. If your FPS drops near farms or in busy villages, lowering this from 12 to 8 helps more than any graphics setting. The trade-off is that far-away farms pause until you get closer.

3. Graphics: Fast

Fancy and Fabulous! draw see-through leaves and sort transparent objects, which costs a lot on integrated and laptop GPUs. Fast makes leaves solid. Most people stop noticing within minutes.

4. Max Framerate and VSync

Check Max Framerate under Video Settings. If it's 60 and your screen is 144 Hz, you're throwing away frames your monitor could show. Set it to your refresh rate, or Unlimited. VSync also caps you at the refresh rate and adds input delay; turn it off if you don't mind occasional tearing, or keep it on if you have a G-Sync/FreeSync screen and prefer smoothness.

5. Smaller wins

  • Clouds: Fast or Off. Fancy 3D clouds are drawn every frame.
  • Particles: Decreased. Helps during explosions, rain and potion effects.
  • Biome Blend: 0 or 1. Faster chunk building, sharper colour edges between biomes.
  • Entity Shadows: Off. Tiny cost each, but there are a lot of mobs.
  • Entity Distance: 75% or 50% in crowded farms.

Things that don't help much

  • Giving Minecraft tons of RAM. Vanilla rarely needs more than 4 GB. Allocating 12 GB+ can make garbage-collection pauses longer, which feels like stutter.
  • Mipmap levels. Small effect on modern GPUs; turning them off makes distant blocks shimmer.
  • FOV. A wider FOV shows a bit more, but the cost is minor compared to render distance.

The biggest upgrade: a performance mod

If you're fine with mods, Sodium (on Fabric) rewrites Minecraft's renderer and often gives far more FPS than every setting above combined. Get it only from its official Modrinth page.
